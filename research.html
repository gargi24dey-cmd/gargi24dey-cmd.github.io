Research

MSc Dissertation

Explainable Machine Learning for Early Prediction of Corporate Sales Slowdowns in Indian Listed Firms

My dissertation examines whether machine learning models can reliably flag an early slowdown in a company's sales before it becomes apparent in headline financial results — and, just as importantly, whether the reasoning behind those predictions can be made transparent enough for practical business or investment use, rather than functioning as a black box.

The analysis draws on CMIE Prowess data covering Indian listed firms, with an initial feasibility study conducted across ten firms spanning five sectors to confirm the availability of profit-and-loss variables at a quarterly frequency and to identify limitations in balance-sheet data. Key methodological questions I'm currently working through include how best to define a "sales slowdown" event, designing a robust walk-forward validation approach suited to time-series financial data, and weighing the trade-offs between quarterly and annual data frequency.
