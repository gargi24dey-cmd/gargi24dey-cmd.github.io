Explainable Machine Learning for Early Prediction of Corporate Sales Slowdowns

An MSc dissertation on Indian listed firms

Project Overview

This dissertation investigates whether machine learning models can predict a slowdown in corporate sales one quarter ahead, using financial information available at the time of prediction. The study focuses on Indian listed non-financial firms and examines both the prediction of future sales growth and the identification of firms experiencing a significant deterioration in sales performance.

The empirical analysis uses quarterly financial data from CMIE Prowess covering 2016 Q1 to 2025 Q4. The research involves constructing a firm-level financial dataset, screening firms for eligibility and data availability, and evaluating multiple regression and classification models under a time-ordered validation framework. Model predictions will be further interpreted using SHAP-based explainable AI techniques to identify the financial factors contributing to predicted sales slowdowns.

A feasibility study across a small set of firms and sectors is being used to assess data availability and the practicality of the proposed methodology before extending the analysis to the broader eligible sample.

Research Design

Data: CMIE Prowess quarterly financial data

Study period: 2016 Q1 – 2025 Q4

Prediction horizon: One quarter ahead

Sample: Indian listed non-financial firms

Regression target: Next-quarter YoY sales growth

Classification target: Significant deterioration in YoY sales growth

Validation: Chronological train–validation–test framework

Interpretability: SHAP-based analysis
