Forecasting UPI Growth in India (2016–2026)

A time-series analysis of India's Unified Payments Interface (UPI) transaction growth, comparing forecasting approaches and examining how reliably they predict transaction volume as growth patterns change.

Project Overview

India's digital payments ecosystem has expanded rapidly over the past decade. However, forecasting transaction activity becomes challenging when growth rates change over time. This project evaluates different forecasting methods to understand which approaches perform more reliably across different periods.

Research Question

How accurately can monthly UPI transaction volume be forecast, and does the performance of forecasting models change across forecast horizons and evaluation periods?

Objectives

  • Study UPI transaction volume and value trends.
  • Examine changes in transaction growth.
  • Compare alternative forecasting models.
  • Evaluate forecast errors using out-of-sample data.
  • Compare transaction volume and value growth.

Dataset and Tools

The analysis uses monthly aggregate UPI statistics published by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI).

Dataset Details

  • Source: NPCI UPI Product Statistics
  • Period: April 2016–June 2026
  • Frequency: Monthly
  • Observations: 123 months
  • Coverage: India, national aggregate
  • Variables: Transaction volume, transaction value, month and derived time-series variables.

Methodology

The project follows a structured time-series workflow, from data preparation and trend analysis to model comparison and out-of-sample forecasting evaluation.

1

Data Cleaning and Preparation

Prepared monthly dates, converted numeric variables, checked missing values and duplicate observations, and constructed growth rates, lag variables and log-transformed series where appropriate.

2

Exploratory Data Analysis

Examined transaction volume and value over time, calculated growth rates, and investigated the relationship between the expansion of UPI and transaction activity.

3

Time-Series Diagnostics

Used the Augmented Dickey–Fuller (ADF) test to assess stationarity and examined potential changes in the growth pattern using structural-change tests.

4

Forecasting Models

Compared naïve forecasting, Simple Exponential Smoothing (SES), Holt's linear trend, damped Holt, recursive linear regression and ARIMA specifications.

5

Forecast Evaluation

Evaluated models using a chronological 12-month hold-out period and rolling-origin evaluation across 14 forecast origins, with a 12-month forecast horizon.

6

Volume and Value Comparison

Compared transaction volume growth with total transaction value growth and examined changes in aggregate average transaction value.

Forecasting Results

Key Findings

The analysis highlights the expansion of UPI, changes in growth patterns, and differences in forecasting performance.

1. UPI transaction volume expanded substantially.

Monthly transaction volume rose from zero in the initial recorded months to more than 22,700 million transactions in June 2026.

2. Growth slowed as the system matured.

Average monthly growth fell from 33.42% in the early launch period to 3.36% during April 2021–June 2026.

3. Holt's linear trend performed best in the evaluation.

It achieved a rolling-origin RMSE of 566.96 million transactions and the lowest RMSE in 13 out of 14 evaluation windows.

4. Forecast errors increased at longer horizons.

Mean absolute error was higher for forecast months 10–12 than for months 1–3 in the evaluated models.

5. Transaction volume grew faster than total value.

From June 2021 to June 2026, volume increased by 709.12%, while transaction value increased by 428.37%. The aggregate average value per transaction declined by 34.7%.

6. Structural changes require careful interpretation.

Potential changes in the series were examined, but changes in reporting methodology and the underlying growth pattern limit the conclusions that can be drawn about their causes.

Limitations

The findings should be interpreted in light of the scope of the dataset and the assumptions behind the forecasting models.

Data Limitations

  • National aggregate data do not capture regional or individual payment behaviour.
  • Reporting methodology changes may affect comparability across periods.
  • Transaction value is nominal and not adjusted for inflation.

Model Limitations

  • Models rely mainly on historical patterns rather than external economic drivers.
  • Rolling-origin windows overlap, so their errors are not independent observations.
  • Past forecast accuracy does not guarantee future performance.

Conclusion

India's UPI transaction volume continued to increase between 2016 and 2026, while its growth rate slowed after the initial expansion phase. Among the evaluated models, Holt's linear trend produced the lowest rolling-origin forecast errors. The results also show that forecasting reliability can vary with the prediction horizon and the period being evaluated.

Overall, the project demonstrates how time-series analysis and systematic forecast evaluation can be used to study the evolution of India's digital payments ecosystem.
