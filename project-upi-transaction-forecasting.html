Forecasting UPI Transaction Growth in India

A time-series approach, 2016–2026

This project examines the growth trajectory of Unified Payments Interface (UPI) transactions in India and evaluates whether historical monthly patterns can be used to forecast near-term transaction volumes, using official monthly statistics published by India's National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI). The project is being developed as part of a classroom data analytics exercise — the baseline model has been built and evaluated, while comparison modelling, diagnostics, and final economic interpretation are ongoing.

Objectives

  • Analyze the long-term growth trend in UPI transaction volume and value since its 2016 launch
  • Identify potential structural shifts in adoption patterns over the study period
  • Develop and compare time-series forecasting models to predict near-term monthly transaction volumes
  • Interpret findings in terms of their relevance to policymakers, banks, and payment service providers

Introduction

UPI has become central to India's digital payments infrastructure since its 2016 introduction, but its growth has not been uniform — shaped by policy initiatives, technological developments, the COVID-19 pandemic, and evolving consumer payment behaviour. Despite UPI's growing importance, limited research simultaneously examines its long-term growth trend, potential structural shifts, and the reliability of forecasting models applied to its transaction data. This project applies time-series forecasting techniques to address that gap using a decade of official monthly data.

Dataset

Monthly UPI statistics published directly by NPCI, covering April 2016 to June 2026 (123 monthly observations), including the number of banks live on UPI, total transaction volume (dependent variable), and total transaction value. Data was obtained from NPCI's official Product Statistics page and combined into a single time series.

Methodology

Data Cleaning

The raw data required correcting Volume and Value fields (stored as text due to comma formatting) and converting the Month field to datetime format. No missing values, duplicates, or invalid observations were found, so no records needed removal.

Feature Engineering

Constructed variables include:

  • volume_lag_1, volume_lag_2 — lagged transaction volume, reflecting the autocorrelated nature of the series
  • log_volume — log-transformed volume, intended to linearize the series' exponential growth pattern
  • year, month_num — extracted to capture trend and potential seasonality

Modelling Approach

Given the time-series nature of the data, observations were split chronologically rather than randomly — the final 12 months were held out as a test set, with the preceding 109 months used for training. Two models are planned for comparison: a baseline Simple Exponential Smoothing model (completed) and a Linear Regression model using lagged variables (planned, not yet implemented). Performance is evaluated using Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) for direct interpretability in transaction-volume units.
